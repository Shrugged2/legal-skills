# Sources

These works shaped the methods in the skills. They are sources on reasoning and advocacy, not legal authority. Every example in the skills is original and fictional. Governing law always comes from the matter under review.

## legal-logic

- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, [Inductive Logic](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/spr2025/entries/logic-inductive/). Comparative support for rival hypotheses.
- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, [Legal Probabilism](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/sum2024/entries/legal-probabilism/), especially the discussion of likelihood ratios. Base rates and the direction of evidential support.
- Linda L. Berger & Kathryn M. Stanchi, *Legal Persuasion*, ch. 11, pp. 88–89. Testing analogies by relationship and purpose, not surface features.

## where-the-argument-breaks

- John Delaney, *Learning Legal Reasoning*, "Briefing and the Case Method," p. 3. Separating the components of an opinion. Basis for check 3.
- Berger & Stanchi, *Legal Persuasion*, ch. 11, pp. 88–89. Resemblance in relationships and purpose. Basis for check 4.
- Bryan A. Garner, *The Winning Brief*, tip 88. Supporting premises and answering serious counterarguments. Basis for checks 6 and 7.

## case-briefing

- Ruth Ann McKinney, *Reading Like a Lawyer*. Active-reading questions used before briefing.
- John Delaney, *Learning Legal Reasoning*. Cases as links in a chain; synthesis by similarities and differences.
- Karl Llewellyn, *The Bramble Bush*. Narrow and broad readings of precedent.
