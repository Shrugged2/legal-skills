# Authority Checklist

Produce a list a person can check by hand against the sources. For every authority, show what the document claims it stands for, exactly where, and what a checker needs to confirm.

## The rule

The proposition column records the document's claim, not the authority's holding. Take it from the document's own words. Never supply, correct, or improve a proposition from your knowledge of the case, even when you believe the document is wrong. If you notice a likely problem, put it in the Notes column, label it as an observation to check, and leave the proposition as the document states it.

This skill does not verify anything. Every row starts unchecked. If the sources are available and the user wants them checked, hand the list to **source-verification**.

## What to capture

Scan the whole document, including footnotes, parentheticals, block quotes, headings, and any appendix. Capture four kinds of reliance:

1. **Cited:** a full or short-form citation, including *id.* and *supra*. Resolve each short form to its authority. If an *id.* is ambiguous, flag it.
2. **Quoted:** language attributed to an authority, with or without a citation.
3. **Named but not cited:** an authority discussed by name ("the *Smith* test") without a citation in that sentence.
4. **Cited within a quotation or parenthetical:** an authority the document relies on secondhand (a case quoting another case, or "(citing X)"). List the inner authority too, and mark that the document relies on it through the outer source.

Include statutes, rules, regulations, and secondary sources unless the user asks for cases only. Group them separately from cases.

Also list **legal assertions with no authority**: sentences stating a rule of law with no citation in the sentence or the surrounding paragraph. These are often where a missing cite or an unsupported claim hides.

## Table

One row per citation instance, grouped under each authority. Put a header line for each authority giving the citation exactly as the document states it, the number of instances, and the pages where it appears.

| # | Location | Signal | Proposition as stated | Pincite | Quoted? | Parenthetical or history | Notes | Checked |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

- **Location:** page and paragraph, footnote number, or heading plus opening words.
- **Signal:** none, *see*, *see also*, *cf.*, *but see*, *e.g.*, and so on. The signal changes what must be checked. No signal means the authority directly states the proposition. *See* means it clearly supports it. *Cf.* means it supports it by analogy.
- **Proposition as stated:** the shortest faithful excerpt or paraphrase of the sentence the citation supports. Quote when paraphrase would lose a qualifier such as "may," "generally," or "as a matter of law." When one citation supports several clauses, say which.
- **Pincite:** as given, or "none" if the document gives no pin page. Missing pincites on quotations are a flag.
- **Quoted?:** yes or no. If yes, the quoted words, so the checker can compare them.
- **Parenthetical or history:** the document's explanatory parenthetical and any stated subsequent history ("rev'd on other grounds").
- **Notes:** your observations for the checker, each labeled as an observation: inconsistent citation forms for the same authority, a short form with no antecedent, a quotation without a pincite, the same authority cited for conflicting propositions, or a document relying on dicta it labels as a holding.
- **Checked:** left blank for the person doing the check.

## After the table

1. **Checking priorities.** List the rows a checker should open first: authorities that carry a dispositive point, direct quotations, citations with no signal for strong propositions, secondhand reliance, and any row with a Note.
2. **Uncited legal assertions,** with locations.
3. **Count:** total authorities, total citation instances, and rows with Notes.

## Format

Default to a table in the reply. For a long document, offer a spreadsheet or a document the user can print and mark up, keeping the same columns so the Checked column survives. Keep the citation text exactly as written in the document, errors included. The point is to check what was filed.

## Handoffs within this suite

- Checking the rows against the sources: **source-verification**.
- Establishing what a key authority actually holds: **case-briefing**.
