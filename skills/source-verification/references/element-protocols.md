# Element Protocols

Full checklists by element type. Run every protocol that applies. Each item resolves to Verified (with location), Contradicted, or Unverified.

## 1. Citations and authorities

- **Existence.** The case, statute, or rule exists as cited. A plausible-looking citation is not evidence of a real one.
- **Reporter and pincite.** Volume, reporter, first page, and pin page are correct, and the pin page exists in that reporter. A page number from one reporter attached to a parallel reporter's citation is a common error.
- **Parallel citations.** Both point to the same opinion.
- **Support.** Open the authority. Does it hold what the document uses it for, or is the document citing dicta, a dissent, a concurrence, or a mischaracterized holding?
- **Subsequent history.** Reversed, vacated, superseded, abrogated, or questioned? An intermediate appellate opinion cited as controlling after a higher court reversed it is the canonical miss. Without a citator, mark Unverified.
- **Short forms.** *Id.*, *supra*, and short cites point to the right antecedent. No dangling *id.* after an intervening citation.
- **Table of authorities.** Every authority in the body appears in the table, every table entry appears in the body, and the page references are right. Flag both directions.
- **Unpublished opinions.** Flag any unpublished or non-precedential opinion cited as precedent where local rules restrict that use.

Typical failures: one case's court and year pasted onto another case's entry; the same case cited correctly once and incorrectly elsewhere; a body citation that never reaches the table.

## 2. Quotations

- **Verbatim.** Compare character by character against the source, not against the document's version.
- **Ellipses and brackets.** An ellipsis may not drop a qualifier that changes the meaning. A bracket may not insert a word the source does not support.
- **Pincite.** The cited page is the page the quotation appears on.
- **Context.** The surrounding passage does not reverse or qualify the quoted words ("the court rejected the argument that [quoted language]").
- **Speaker.** In record quotations, the right witness or speaker is credited.

## 3. Numbers and damages

- **Tie-out.** Every figure traces to a specific source document. Note the source beside each.
- **Independent recompute.** Recompute every total and subtotal from the line items. Do not rely on the document's formulas.
- **Reconciliation.** Subtotals add to the grand total; a figure that appears twice matches in both places.
- **Double counting.** The same charge is not captured under two labels.
- **Scope.** Only in-scope items are included. Watch both directions: a dropped row undercounts, and an unrelated charge from the same provider overcounts.
- **Method.** Adjustments (write-offs, percentages, liens) are applied on the stated basis to every row. Flag an inconsistency as a question; do not silently correct it.
- **Units and rounding.** Dollars and cents, percentages and decimals, consistent rounding.

## 4. Factual assertions

- **Traceability.** Every factual sentence maps to a record source. A sentence with no possible record cite is a flag.
- **Characterization versus fact.** "He was obviously impaired" is a characterization. "He registered 0.18" is an observed fact. Report the difference; leave to the writer whether to keep the characterization.
- **Certainty.** Nothing is stated more strongly than the record supports. "The video shows X" when the footage is ambiguous is a flag.
- **Internal consistency.** Dates, sequences, and actors agree across the document and with the record.

## 5. Record cites and page mappings

- **Source of the mapping.** A mapping between transcript pages and an appendix or exhibit packet comes from the actual page markers, not interpolation.
- **Continuity.** Ranges are continuous and do not overlap.
- **Cross-references.** A cite to another witness's testimony resolves to the right page.
- **Dual citations.** Where a cite gives both transcript and appendix pages, both halves are right everywhere the passage appears.

## 6. Dates and deadlines

- **Trigger date.** The date the clock runs from is itself sourced.
- **Rule.** The period is named with its authority.
- **Computation.** The deadline is calculated visibly, accounting for tolling and for weekend and holiday rules where they apply.
- **Agreement.** A deadline that appears in several places matches.

## 7. Identifiers

- **Exact match.** Party names, caption, court, cause number, bar numbers, addresses, and contact details match the source exactly.
- **Transpositions.** Check every character of phone, bar, and cause numbers.
- **Signature blocks.** Consistent with the source and with the other blocks in the document.
- **Placeholders.** No leftover "INSERT" text or half-filled fields.
